ATS-friendly resume: how to get past the automatic filter

Many companies use applicant tracking systems (ATS) that read and rank resumes before a person sees them. If the software can’t understand your resume, it can end up at the bottom of the list even if you fit the job.

Updated: November 24, 2026

In this guide
  1. What an ATS is
  2. Formatting that ATS read well
  3. Keywords from the job posting
  4. How to check your resume

1. What an ATS is

An applicant tracking system is the software a company uses to manage its openings: it receives resumes, extracts their text (name, job titles, dates, education, skills) and lets recruiters search and filter candidates. Everyone from large companies to job boards uses them.

The key point: an ATS works with the text of your resume. Anything it can’t extract as text doesn’t exist for it.

2. Formatting that ATS read well

  • A single column is the safest choice. Some systems read columns mixed together.
  • Standard section headings: “Work Experience”, “Education”, “Skills”, “Languages”. A creative heading (“My Journey”) may not be recognized.
  • Real text, not images: no logo with your name, no screenshots of diplomas. Decorative icons are fine if the information is written next to them.
  • Contact details in the body of the document, not in the Word header or footer, which some systems ignore.
  • Clear, consistent dates (“Mar 2021 – Present”).
  • A PDF made from text, not a scan. If you can select and copy the words when you open it, an ATS can read them too.
  • Avoid complex tables, text boxes and “skill level” graphics (bars or stars): they carry no readable information.

3. Keywords from the job posting

Recruiters often search the ATS for terms from the posting: the job title, tools, degrees or certifications. If your resume doesn’t contain them, you won’t show up in those searches.

  1. Read the posting and underline concrete requirements: “Salesforce”, “customer service”, “forklift certification”, “fluent Spanish”.
  2. Check which ones you really have and write them with the same words as the posting, in your summary, experience or skills.
  3. Include both the acronym and the full form when there is one: “Search Engine Optimization (SEO)”.

Don’t stuff your resume with keywords you can’t back up: getting past the filter only helps if you can prove it in the interview.

4. How to check your resume

  • Open the PDF, select everything (Ctrl+A or Cmd+A) and paste it into a plain text editor. If the text comes out complete and in order, you’re fine.
  • Make sure your name, email and phone number appear as text.
  • Give the file a clear name: First_Last_Resume.pdf.
