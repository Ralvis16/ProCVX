How to write a resume summary (10 examples)

The resume summary is the two or three lines under your name. It’s the first thing people read, so it has to answer one question fast: why should I keep reading this resume?

Updated: November 24, 2026

In this guide
  1. A three-sentence formula
  2. 10 resume summary examples
  3. How to tailor it to each job
  4. What to avoid

1. A three-sentence formula

  1. Who you are: your job title or qualification and, if you have them, your years of experience.
  2. What you do well: two or three specialties, tools, or an achievement with a number.
  3. What you want: the kind of role or company you are aiming for.

Write it without pronouns (“Lab technician with…”) and keep the same voice throughout. 40 to 70 words is plenty.

GenericProactive, results-driven professional with strong teamwork skills.
SpecificB2B sales rep with 6 years selling industrial supplies. Grew my territory’s client base by 30% in two years. Looking for a key account manager role.

2. 10 resume summary examples

No experience (recent graduate)
Marketing and communications graduate with an internship at a digital agency, where I managed social media for three clients. Skilled in Meta Business Suite, Canva and Google Analytics. Looking for my first role on a digital marketing team.

Student
Second-year IT support student (graduating June 2026) with hands-on experience building PCs and helping users during my internship. Available for afternoon and weekend shifts.

Administration
Office administrator with 5 years of experience in bookkeeping and invoicing for small businesses. Advanced Excel and QuickBooks; handled quarterly tax filings. Looking for a stable role in a finance department.

Customer service
Call center agent with 3 years of customer support and issue resolution for a telecom provider, averaging 4.7/5 in customer ratings. Bilingual English–Spanish. Interested in tier-2 support roles.

Hospitality
Server with 4 years of floor and bar experience in restaurants of up to 120 covers. Food handler certified, with cocktail training. Looking for a steady role in a restaurant or hotel.

Retail
Sales associate with experience in fashion and electronics stores: register, restocking, visual merchandising and consultative selling. Beat my monthly sales target in 9 of the last 12 months.

Logistics
Warehouse associate with a forklift certification and 7 years of order picking with RF scanners and WMS. Used to rotating shifts and peak-season volume.

Healthcare
Certified nursing assistant with 2 years on an internal medicine ward and experience in elder care. Trained in safe patient handling and first aid.

Tech
Web developer with 4 years of experience in JavaScript, React and Node.js. Led the migration of an online store with 50,000 monthly users. Looking for a role on a product team.

Career change
After 8 years as a high school teacher, I’ve specialized in corporate training with a course in instructional design. Experienced in building content, public speaking and measuring results. Looking for a corporate trainer role.

3. How to tailor it to each job

The summary is the part of your resume most worth changing from one application to the next:

  • Use the same job title as the posting if it matches your experience.
  • Lead with the tools or requirements the posting mentions. That also helps you get past ATS filters.
  • Change the last sentence (“what you want”) to suit the kind of company.

4. What to avoid

  • Adjectives without proof: “dynamic”, “proactive”, “self-starter”. Replace them with a fact.
  • Objectives that are only about you (“seeking to develop my career”). Say what you bring.
  • Paragraphs longer than four lines. If it can’t be read at a glance, it won’t be read.
  • Personal details (age, marital status, photo in many countries): they add nothing and can work against you.
