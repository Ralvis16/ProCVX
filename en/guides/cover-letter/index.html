How to write a cover letter (with an example)

A cover letter doesn’t repeat your resume: it explains why you fit this job at this company. Done well, it takes a minute to read and sets you apart from everyone who only attached a resume.

Updated: November 24, 2026

In this guide
  1. When to send one
  2. The four-paragraph structure
  3. A full example
  4. If you send it by email
  5. Common mistakes

1. When to send one

Send one whenever the posting asks for it, and also in these cases:

  • Speculative applications: you write to a company that hasn’t posted a job. Without a letter, nobody knows what role you want.
  • Changing careers, or a gap in your history that is worth explaining in one sentence.
  • Small companies or niche roles, where the person hiring usually reads everything.

On job boards with an optional cover letter field, a few tailored lines are worth more than leaving it empty or pasting a generic text.

2. The four-paragraph structure

You don’t need more than a page; 200 to 300 words is ideal.

  1. Greeting and purpose. Address a person by name if you know it. Say which job you are applying for and where you saw it.
  2. Why you fit. Pick two or three requirements from the posting and show you meet them with a fact or an achievement.
  3. Why this company. One or two sentences that prove you know it: a product, a project, the way they work.
  4. Closing. Availability, an invitation to interview and a sign-off.
GenericI am writing to offer my services, as I am a hard-working person who is eager to learn.
SpecificI’m applying for the Administrative Assistant role posted on Indeed. During my internship at an accounting firm I processed over 200 invoices a month in QuickBooks, one of the tools you list.

3. A full example

Dear Ms. Garcia,

I’m writing to apply for the Sales Associate position at your downtown store, posted on your website last week.

For the last two summers I worked as a sales associate at a sporting goods store serving more than 300 customers a day, where I handled the register, restocking, and returns. During peak season I suggested a queue system for the fitting rooms that cut rush-hour lines noticeably. I also speak fluent Spanish, which helps in a neighborhood with so many visitors.

I know your brand as a customer and I especially like the personal service your staff gives. I’d love to bring that same care to your team.

I’m available to start right away and have a flexible schedule. I’d be glad to meet for an interview whenever it suits you.

Best regards,
Laura Martinez · (555) 123-4567 · laura.martinez@email.com

4. If you send it by email

  • Clear subject line: “Application: Sales Associate, Downtown – Laura Martinez”.
  • You can write the letter in the body of the email (easier to read) and attach your resume as a PDF.
  • Check the company’s and the person’s name before sending: it’s the easiest mistake to make when you reuse a letter.

Tip: make sure what you say in the letter also appears in your resume. With ProCVX you can tailor your summary and experience to each job and export it to PDF in a minute.

5. Common mistakes

  • Opening with “To whom it may concern” and a filler sentence. Get to the point in the first line.
  • Rewriting your resume as paragraphs. The letter selects and explains; it doesn’t list.
  • Talking only about what you want (“I’m looking to grow”) and not about what you bring.
  • Sending the same letter to every company. It shows.
