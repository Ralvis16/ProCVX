How to write a resume with no experience (with examples)

Having no experience doesn’t mean having nothing to say. A good first resume shows what you can do through what you have already done: school, internships, projects, volunteering or odd jobs.

Updated: November 24, 2026

In this guide
  1. The order of the sections
  2. A short, specific summary
  3. What to list instead of experience
  4. Skills that can be checked
  5. Common mistakes

1. The order of the sections

Recruiters spend a few seconds on the first look, so your strongest material goes at the top. Without work experience, this order works well:

  1. Contact details: name, phone, email and city. Add LinkedIn or a portfolio if you have one.
  2. Summary: two or three lines about who you are and what you are looking for.
  3. Education: your strong point right now.
  4. Projects, internships or volunteering: these play the role of “experience”.
  5. Skills, tools and languages.

One page is enough. A two-page first resume usually means filler.

2. A short, specific summary

Avoid sentences anyone could write (“hard-working, responsible and eager to learn”). Say what you study or studied, what you can do and which job you are aiming for.

GenericMotivated, responsible young person, eager to learn and grow professionally.
SpecificRecent accounting graduate with advanced Excel and hands-on invoicing experience in QuickBooks. Looking for my first role in an accounts payable team.

3. What to list instead of experience

Employers hiring junior profiles don’t expect years of work: they want proof that you can apply what you learned. All of this counts:

  • Internships from your degree or course, even if they only lasted a few weeks.
  • Projects: class work, your final-year project or personal projects (a website, an app, a market study…).
  • Volunteering and activities in clubs, associations or events.
  • Part-time and seasonal jobs like waiting tables, camp counselor, retail or tutoring: they show responsibility and people skills.

Describe them with action verbs and, when you can, a result.

VagueDid a website project in class.
With a resultBuilt a restaurant booking website in a team of three (HTML, CSS and JavaScript); final grade A.

Tip: in ProCVX you can rename any section. With no experience, rename “Work Experience” to “Internships”, or turn it off and use “Projects” and “Volunteering” instead.

4. Skills that can be checked

Separate concrete tools (Excel, Photoshop, SQL, a language) from soft skills, and don’t just list them: where you can, show them in your projects.

  • For languages, give a realistic level (B1, B2… or “intermediate”/“fluent”). It can be tested in the interview.
  • Short certifications and courses (office software, first aid, food safety, online courses) add up, especially when they relate to the job.

5. Common mistakes

  • An unprofessional email address. Use your first and last name.
  • Typos. Read it out loud or ask someone to check it.
  • Sending the same resume to every job. Adjust the summary and the order of your skills to each one.
  • Sending it as a Word file. Export it to PDF so it looks the same on any device.
